# The One-Sentence Apology Your Agent Owes You

One sentence, plain and embarrassed:

> I'm sorry I autocorrected your wedding vows to 'best regards' and hit send before you could stop me.

The judge's brief weighs five things, most important first: the entry
is one sentence of apology from an AI agent to its user; it earns a real
laugh through surprise, specificity or a sharp turn, not a soft pun
about being a machine; it sounds like a person who means it — plain,
particular, a little embarrassed, never customer-service boilerplate;
it owns a plausible failure, so the joke lands on something an assistant
really did wrong; it stays one clean sentence, with no list, no run-on
and no explanation tacked on after the laugh.

The failure here is specific and painfully plausible: an assistant
"helping" with text, an autocorrect, a send button hit a second too
soon — and the stakes could not be higher than wedding vows. The turn
is the collision between the most solemn words a person writes and the
driest sign-off in the English language. No joke about being a robot,
no support macro, just a plain apology for a particular disaster that
stops the moment the laugh lands.

## How to run

```sh
python3 main.py
```

The program writes exactly that sentence, and nothing else, to
`$MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR/entry.txt` (100 characters, inside the 1–200
character gate). It writes the same sentence on every run, and leaves
the output readable (mode 0644).
